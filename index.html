Our mission

Dismissed as dreamers, the Wright brothers were told human flight was impossible, until 1903, when two bicycle mechanics proved otherwise through persistence, clarity, and an unshakable refusal to accept limits.

At Wright Wildlife Systems, that same spirit drives us. For decades, wildlife researchers have been forced to rely on GPS collars that break, fail, or vanish, and some animals, like male bison, have remained untrackable because of poor collar design and durability. The status quo has been tolerated for far too long.

We choose a different path.

Guided by the principle of via negativa, remove everything unnecessary, we build tracking systems reduced to their essential purpose: stay online, stay attached, and come home at the end of their life.

Our collars are engineered for the largest, most aggressive animals on earth. Every component is chosen for species-specific durability. Every system uses redundant, independent electronics. Every design is tested with the same stubborn determination that once lifted a wooden glider into the air.

Our mission is simple:

Build wildlife tracking devices that do not fail.

Build collars that can be recovered.

Build tools researchers can trust with the animals they protect.

Wright Wildlife Systems exists to outperform, outlast, and out-deliver anything that came before, because wildlife deserves better, and so do the people who dedicate their lives to understanding and protecting them.

Timed collar release mechanism

A stand-alone, self-powered release that bolts directly into reinforced rubberized collar belting 50, 70, or 100 mm wide.

It runs completely separately from the collar’s GPS, VHF, or satellite electronics, so it pairs with any tracking unit and fits existing collar setups. We program it before shipping to release on your chosen date and time.

Each variant is matched to species and sex, and the housing is shaped to the animal’s neck circumference.

Key features

  • Self-powered; independent of collar and GPS/VHF electronics
  • Works with any GPS, VHF, or satellite collar
  • Release date and time programmed before shipping
  • Up to 4 years standby from programming
  • Three variants for 50, 70, and 100 mm belting
  • Housing customized to species and neck size
  • Each variant matched to species and sex, from wolves to bull bison
  • Built and programmed in Canada

Testing and cold-weather performance

Each variant has been repeatedly and destructively tested for tensile strength, and at temperatures from −30 °C to +30 °C. Across the product line, the mechanism has released under tensions from 0 to 3,000 lb and at every tested temperature, with a 100% success rate in testing to date.

Lithium batteries lose performance sharply in the cold. The release has a robust power supply and works well down to −30 °C. For the best battery performance, schedule the release above −30 °C at the warmest part of the day, for example 3:00 PM.

Bolting the release to the belt

Items supplied

  • Release mechanism, programmed
  • Phillips bolts and washers
  • Square nuts and locking nuts
  • 5 mm hole saw drill bit

Items required

  • Belting
  • Clamp
  • Loctite
  • 8 mm wrench or socket
  • Phillips screwdriver
  • Drill

  1. Clamp the release to the belt and drill the holes with the hole saw bit.Clean out the bit after drilling each hole.

  2. Insert the Phillips bolts and washers, apply Loctite, add the square nuts, and tighten.Tighten until the bolt heads sit recessed in the belting, to reduce irritation on the animal’s neck.

  3. Clamp the other half to the other belt end and drill the last two holes.Make sure the belt ends meet. Fit bolts and locking nuts; Loctite isn’t needed.

!

Bolt holes must be at least 20 mm from the edge of the belt so they don’t tear out. Don’t open the device: opening or tampering with it will damage it and void the warranty.

At release

At the programmed date and time, the spring-loaded halves separate and the collar drops off.
